# Speaker briefing — German naturalness review

You are checking whether German sentences in a learning app sound like something a real
speaker would say. The app's own tests already prove the exercises work; what they cannot
prove is naturalness — that is exactly what your ear adds.

- Age band (under 25 / 25–40 / 41–60 / over 60):

- Grew up abroad in another language? (yes / no):

Different regions and generations disagree — that disagreement is data, not noise.

## The one question — and what it is not

For every row, answer in your own words: **"Would you say this? And if not — what would you say?"**

- Answer in **free text**. The second column is where the value is; a yes/no answer is noise.
- Never asked to judge grammar rules — judge sentences. If a sentence is unnatural, write the
  one you would actually say.
- Where you can, imagine the situation the sentence lives in (ordering food, asking a friend,
  describing yesterday) and answer as if you were in it. If you cannot imagine any situation
  where someone would say it, say so — that is a finding.

## The rules that make this reliable

1. **Situations, not opinions.** "Your friend says 'see you next week', you're reminding them
   about your party tomorrow — what do you say?" The answer you want is the sentence, not a
   verdict about the sentence.
2. **The hypothesis is withheld.** Nobody will tell you what we suspect about a row, because
   people look for affirmation of the asker's theory. Answer before asking us anything.
3. **One voice is never enough.** The same grids go to 2–3 speakers from different regions and
   generations. A single "wrong" is worth very little; a pattern across speakers is worth a lot.
4. **Mind the dialect.** Say plainly when something is correct in your region but not another,
   or common in your generation but not others — do not silently "correct" it to your own.
5. **False natives.** If you grew up abroad in another language, be extra careful: confident
   corrections from negative transfer are the classic failure of this kind of review. The header
   fields above let us weigh your answers honestly.
6. **Watch for imagined context.** If you "know" what a sentence must mean, you may approve it
   for that meaning alone. We will re-ask with a different sentence carrying the same structure
   to see whether your verdict holds.

## What happens to your answers

Your answers are collected as **suspects to discuss**, not bugs to auto-fix. Rows flagged by
several speakers get talked through with context; rows flagged once usually stay untouched.

## Practicalities

- Work one `<bucket>.md` file at a time (one structure per file), or `all-sentences.csv` if you
  prefer a spreadsheet. Fill the two free-text columns; leave natural rows blank.
- Rows in *Mixed prose (teaching text)* are whole teaching paragraphs — check the German embedded
  in the English prose, not the English around it.
- Rows show a source like `1103 · dictation · spoken` (lesson · surface) — quote the row id when
  a sentence needs discussion.
